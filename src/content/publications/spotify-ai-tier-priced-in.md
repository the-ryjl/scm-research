> **Position:** Short SPOT at approximately $499.94, with a $369.28 price target—roughly 26% below the July 31 close. The bear, base, and bull values are $308.56, $369.28, and $538.80, respectively.

Spotify is a global audio platform with 751 million monthly active users and approximately 290 million Premium subscribers. Since the May 21 Investor Day, the stock has re-rated roughly 25% on an AI “Super Pro-Tier” narrative and a 35–40% gross-margin guide. We believe that is a multiple-expansion story rather than an earnings story.

Our 261-respondent paid-subscriber survey implies monthly churn is running approximately 50 basis points above Street estimates as the latest price hike takes hold. Free-to-paid conversion is decelerating, while North America is already highly penetrated. Even giving the Pro Tier full credit, our DCF arrives at $401; our primary valuation points to $369.28.

## Key points

1. **What we are short:** SPOT at approximately $500. Our $369.28 target applies a 32x multiple to FY26 adjusted EPS of $11.54, with an approximately 8x reward-to-risk ratio.
2. **The setup:** The stock has re-rated approximately 25% since the May 21 Investor Day on Pro Tier expectations. The average buyer since then sits near $501, versus a volume-weighted average price of $516, creating a high bar for the next earnings print.
3. **What the Street misses:** Consensus is underwriting “multiple independent vectors of pricing.” Our survey found that 57% of subscribers would consider switching after another price hike, while churn already appears 30–50 basis points above Street expectations.
4. **What we expect:** The Q2 and Q3 prints should show the price hike affecting churn rather than ARPU. We expect Street FY27 EPS of $15.97 to move toward our $14.40 estimate as the market discounts vague Pro Tier disclosures.
5. **Valuation:** Our $369.28 target uses a 32x multiple on FY26 adjusted EPS. DCF cross-checks are $349 standalone and $401 with full Pro Tier credit. Our bear case is $308.56 at 28x; our bull case is $538.80 at 40x.

**In a sentence:** An “AI tier” growth narrative drove multiple expansion despite decelerating free-to-paid conversion, accelerating price-hike churn, and a mispriced Super Pro-Tier NPV. We see approximately 30% downside.

## Business overview and stock dynamics

Spotify makes money mainly through Premium subscriptions—where users pay monthly for ad-free listening, downloads, on-demand playback, and higher audio quality—and through advertising, where brands pay to reach free users through audio, video, display, and podcast ads.

Since the Investor Day, Spotify has traded up approximately 25%. Management highlighted two ideas: a new Pro Tier that would give “super fans” access to new AI features, and gross-margin guidance of 35–40% through FY30.

### The Street view

The Street is underwriting SPOT as a cleaner compounder following Investor Day. Management offered a tangible bridge to mid-teens revenue growth, 35–40% gross margin, and 20%+ operating margins. The bull case is that Spotify now has more ways to beat expectations through add-ons, AI music, Marketplace, biddable ads, and buybacks.

**The good:** ARPU could gain more layers. Barclays calls these “multiple independent vectors of pricing.” Deutsche Bank argues that SPOT can monetize super-users with higher engagement and willingness to pay. Potential attachments include audiobooks, AI remixing, podcast memberships, ticketing, and higher-usage tiers. If attach rates flow through this year, Premium ARPU may be understated.

**The bad:** Much of this remains theory. Add-ons need real attachment, AI inference costs and label splits need to remain contained, and Marketplace and ads need to become large enough to matter. Spotify's taste-graph moat needs to show up in churn and pricing, not just in the product narrative.

A former Senior Business Intelligence Analyst at Spotify said the saturation thesis was “somewhat valid,” adding that Spotify has had to look internationally for much of its growth, redo the free plan, and seek other ways to monetize.

## Debunking the Street: drivers of variance

### Claim 1: Premium user growth will remain in line, reflecting industry pricing power

Sentiment toward the price hike has been positive from both management and the Street. We see the hike as an obstacle to Premium growth. With low switching costs and increasing competition, it is difficult to see Spotify as the ultimate winner in mature regions.

North America and Europe—historically the largest contributors to revenue—are becoming more saturated. This leaves Spotify's growth increasingly reliant on international markets where pricing power is lower and piracy and lower-cost providers are more prevalent.

An expert call reinforced the point: “The land-grab phase is over.” Future growth comes from consumers buying their first subscription and from entering new markets where pricing power is much lower.

North American saturation is not merely a cap on growth; it is also an inherent risk to the existing Premium base. Spotify serves 58% of paid subscribers in Canada and the United States, with only approximately 3.5% of additional paid users left to capture. Year-over-year growth has decelerated for three years, and Q1 2026 showed a faster slowdown than we expected from a dominant platform.

Customers value Spotify's application, interface, convenience, and music accessibility. But as competing products such as Apple Music and YouTube Music improve, Spotify is raising prices and adding AI features that many users do not use. One diligence-call participant said that they use almost none of the add-ons introduced over the past year and are finding it harder to justify the higher price.

### Claim 2: ARPU compounds as users stack multiple add-ons

The Street assumes features such as AI remixes, Audiobooks Plus, and Reserved Ticketing will be margin-accretive in the near term. The Super Pro-Tier would represent a meaningful change in the consumer-subscription model. Music, however, is a relatively fixed product sold across multiple streaming services with similar distribution formats, leaving the user's incremental willingness to pay uncertain.

These add-ons require compute resources and licensing expense and may not improve conversion. New features could therefore compress near-term margins. We do not believe Spotify deserves its post-Investor Day multiple when there are no prior comparable products to support the Pro Tier revenue acceleration the Street is modeling.

Bulls argue that AI features will benefit from Spotify's user-data flywheel. Yet other consumer-internet companies—including Pinterest, Roblox, and Snapchat—have found that abundant user data alone is not enough to persuade users to spend more on AI features.

Our paid poll of more than 250 participants found that only 16% care about the technology features offered to Premium users; the rest primarily value familiarity and the simple interface. Assuming 17.5% of Premium users are “superfans,” a generous $6 price delta, and a 13% adoption rate, we estimate $3.664 billion of additional annual revenue in FY30. Even with those assumptions, our DCF still implies 24% downside. A regional mix-shift that reduces the price delta to $4 adds approximately five percentage points to the downside.

We assume a 40% incremental gross margin for the Pro Tier, despite significant uncertainty about royalties and music costs. Standard music-streaming royalties are approximately 75% of revenue to labels. If labels successfully claim incremental royalties, gross margin for the segment could fall to 20–25%.

## The two drivers of variance

### Driver 1: Pricing power has hit a ceiling

**Qualitatively:** Street models assume continued pricing power through FY27–28, citing the successful 2024–25 price hikes as evidence that current and future increases will stick. The Street expects price increases in North America and Europe to keep ARPU high and protect it from geographic mix-shift; rest-of-world ARPU is approximately one-third of North American ARPU.

**Channel checks:** Our 261-respondent survey of paid users in the United States and Canada, conducted through Prolific, produced 256 valid completes. It indicates that pricing power is structurally exhausted at $12.99.

Half of respondents questioned the latest price hike and were considering switching—or had already switched. We estimate that churn in the coming quarter will be approximately 50 basis points above Street expectations and remain at least 30 basis points above expectations for the rest of the year.

Retention may be manageable, but acquisition is a larger problem when competitors offer subscriptions at approximately a 15% monthly discount. Spotify may not convert free users at the previous rate while churn remains elevated. Even if rest-of-world growth makes up for missing Premium conversions in mature regions, ARPU will suffer from geographic mix.

Another price hike this year or early next year could create significant backlash. Our poll suggests churn could be 25% higher, adding another 85 basis points of monthly churn. We therefore see further near-term price increases as unlikely and future ARPU dilution as difficult to avoid.

### Driver 2: The Super Pro-Tier is an AI “Hail Mary”

**Qualitatively:** Since the Super Pro-Tier was introduced at Investor Day, the stock has re-rated by more than $100, or approximately 24%. Deutsche Bank models €1–4 billion in incremental revenue and gross margin of 37.5–42.5%. The Pro Tier is the largest growth narrative behind the move, but we believe the long-term cash flows it can produce do not justify the valuation.

**Quantitatively:** We estimate that only 17.5% of users care meaningfully about the proposed AI and technology features. At our projected rates, the tier could generate $3.4–4.2 billion of recurring annual revenue by FY30. With approximately 40% incremental gross margin, our base-case DCF implies a value of $401. Our most bullish adoption and revenue projections produce a fair value of approximately $480, still below the $499.94 July 31 close.

### Sensitivity analysis

1. **Regional pricing:** Bloomberg Intelligence models $6/$4/$3/$1 regional pricing versus our flat $6 assumption. If mature regions find the offering less compelling while the rest of the world adopts at lower prices, a $4 average delta adds approximately five percentage points to our downside.
2. **Adoption curve:** Spotify launched more than eight AI features in 18 months, including AI DJ, AI Playlist, SongDNA, Taste Profile, Prompted Playlist, About the Song, generative-AI ads, and voice translation. Premium user growth continued to decline. A fall in the FY30 adoption rate to 10% adds approximately five percentage points to our downside.
3. **Comparable AI monetization:** Pinterest launched AI-powered visual search and personalization without measurable 2024–25 ARPU lift. Snapchat's My AI reached hundreds of millions of users without a monetization breakthrough, and Roblox's AI creator tools did not produce a measurable revenue impact. These examples reduce our confidence that Spotify will execute at the best possible outcome.
4. **Cost of revenue:** Standard music-streaming royalties pass through approximately 70% of revenue to labels. UMG and WMG have framed AI rights as growth vectors and may seek royalties on incremental AI-music revenue. We also estimate AI compute load at $1–2 per month per Pro Tier user, depending on usage. A margin below 40% would pressure Spotify's FY30 gross-margin guidance.

## Margin story

Our model sits modestly below the Street on margins through FY30, with explicit quarterly dynamics in FY26 driving the near-term variance. Q2 and Q3 2026 carry elevated R&D and sales-and-marketing expense—approximately a $250 million sequential step-up—with R&D rising from $331 million to $470 million and sales and marketing rising from $449 million to $559 million quarter over quarter. This is tied to management's approximately $200 million of flagged incremental operating expense. We then model operating expense normalizing gradually into Q2 2027.

For Premium and Ad-Supported cost of goods sold, we are roughly in line with the Street. Most of our variance comes from lower revenue and user-growth estimates. Royalties and podcasts should remain the largest costs regardless of an incremental two percentage points of revenue. We estimate approximately 36% gross margin in our base projections and approximately 37.5% with the Pro Tier.

## Optionality

Spotify reports in euros but trades in U.S. dollars. If the dollar strengthens by 5–10%, reported euro-translated revenue from U.S. subscribers—approximately 40% of revenue—gets a tailwind, but foreign-exchange-neutral growth optically decelerates. We do not include this in our base case, but it is worth monitoring.

## Risks

### 1. Add-on attachment materially exceeds expectations

Our base case assumes audiobooks, AI remixing, podcast memberships, ticketing, and higher-usage tiers are incremental rather than transformational. If high-engagement users stack multiple paid features and attach rates flow through to ARPU by FY26 or FY27, the market will underwrite a higher lifetime value per subscriber and the stock will likely re-rate.

**Mitigant:** We are watching for actual paid attachment and ARPU flow-through, not engagement language. Without disclosed conversion or revenue contribution, the risk remains more narrative than numbers.

### 2. AI remixing becomes a paid product with clean economics

The UMG deal is the largest product risk to the short. If Spotify proves AI music can be margin-neutral or accretive, the bear argument around rights risk, inference cost, and label splits weakens. Our model assumes AI is not a major near-term P&L contributor and could create cost leakage if usage scales.

**Mitigant:** Spotify must show real margin drop-through after label splits, artist payouts, and compute costs. Until then, we do not give full credit for AI revenue.

### 3. Advertising reaccelerates faster than modeled

Spotify has guided to second-half 2026 advertising growth acceleration and double-digit growth beyond. If biddable advertising continues to gain share, the ad business could shift from a drag to a margin-accretive growth lever. Our model assumes ads remain choppy and too small to drive the consolidated story.

**Mitigant:** Advertising is still a smaller bottom-line contributor, so the risk becomes thesis-breaking only if acceleration is sustained and accompanied by clear margin improvement.

## Catalysts and path to getting paid

### Q2/Q3 2026 earnings prints

The next prints provide the first clean read on whether the latest price hike is sticking or driving churn. If Premium subscribers are in line but ARPU decelerates because of lower-ARPU geographies, the stock is likely to de-rate. Street estimates should move lower if churn exceeds expectations, free-to-paid conversion continues to decelerate, and management relies on international growth to offset weaker adoption in mature markets.

### Super-Premium / Pro Tier launch details

The stock already prices in the Pro Tier narrative without evidence of attachment, pricing, or margin contribution. The next catalyst is concrete disclosure about launch timing, price points, regional rollout, and early uptake. Vague engagement language in place of paid attachment, ARPU lift, or revenue contribution would lead investors to discount the Pro Tier NPV.

### AI remix and label-economics disclosure

UMG AI remixing is the largest bull-case product catalyst, but the economics remain unclear. The key question is how much incremental revenue Spotify retains after label splits, artist payments, and compute costs. If labels claim a large share of AI revenue or usage carries meaningful inference cost, the Pro Tier margin thesis—and the 35–40% gross-margin story—weakens.
